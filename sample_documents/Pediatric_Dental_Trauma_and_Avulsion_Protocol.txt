CLINICAL DENTAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE: PEDIATRIC DENTAL TRAUMA & TOOTH AVULSION MANAGEMENT
International Association of Dental Traumatology (IADT) Reference Guidelines
Guideline Code: DENT-TRAUMA-2024-V2

1. GENERAL ASSESSMENT & TRIAGE
- Immediate Neurological & Medical Screen:
  * Check for concussion, loss of consciousness, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, or neck pain. If present, refer immediately to emergency department.
  * Check tetanus immunization status. If the patient has not had a booster within 5 years or wound is contaminated with dirt/soil, administer tetanus prophylaxis within 48 hours.

2. TOOTH AVULSION PROTOCOLS
A. Primary Teeth (Deciduous Dentition) - AGES 0 TO 6 YEARS:
- CRITICAL RULE: NEVER REPLANT AN AVULSED PRIMARY TOOTH!
- Replantation of primary teeth can damage the developing permanent tooth germ beneath the socket (causing enamel hypoplasia, dilaceration, or arrest of permanent tooth eruption).
- Protocol: Confirm tooth has been completely expelled (take radiograph if aspirated or intruded). Reassure parents, prescribe soft diet for 7-10 days, monitor for gingival healing.

B. Permanent Teeth (Dentition in Children & Adults):
- Emergency First Aid at Scene of Injury:
  * Hold tooth only by the crown; NEVER touch or scrape the root.
  * If dirty, rinse gently under cold running water for 10 seconds (do not scrub).
  * Immediately replant into the socket if possible, and have patient bite gently on a clean handkerchief or gauze.
- Storage & Transport Media (when immediate replantation not possible):
  1. Hank's Balanced Salt Solution (HBSS) - Gold standard, maintains periodontal ligament (PDL) cell viability for up to 24 hours.
  2. Cold Bovine Milk - Readily available, optimal osmolarity, keeps cells viable up to 6 hours.
  3. Saliva (buccal vestibule or spit into a container) - Viable for 1-2 hours.
  4. Physiological Saline.
  * WARNING: NEVER store avulsed tooth in tap water (hypotonic, causes rapid cell lysis).
- Clinical Treatment at Dental Office:
  * Extra-oral dry time < 60 minutes:
    - Rinse root gently with saline. Irrigate socket gently with saline to remove blood clot.
    - Replant tooth with gentle finger pressure.
    - Apply flexible splint (0.016" stainless steel wire with composite resin) for 2 WEEKS.
    - Rigid splinting is contraindicated (promotes replacement resorption / ankylosis).
  * Extra-oral dry time > 60 minutes:
    - PDL cells are non-viable. Ankylosis and root resorption are inevitable.
    - Remove necrotic PDL tissue, soak in 2% sodium fluoride for 20 minutes to slow resorption.
    - Replant and place flexible splint for 4 WEEKS.
- Systemic Medications & Prophylaxis:
  * Antibiotic Therapy:
    - For children > 12 years / adults: Doxycycline 100 mg BID for 7 days.
    - For children < 12 years (risk of tooth discoloration with tetracyclines): Amoxicillin 50 mg/kg/day divided TID for 7 days (or Clindamycin if penicillin-allergic).
  * Chlorhexidine 0.12% mouth rinse BID for 2 weeks to assist oral hygiene.
- Endodontic Follow-up:
  * Teeth with closed apex: Initiate root canal therapy within 7 to 10 days after replantation (before splint removal) to prevent inflammatory root resorption. Place Ca(OH)2 paste.
  * Teeth with open apex (immature root): Monitor for revascularization; delay endodontics unless symptoms or radiographic signs of necrosis appear.
